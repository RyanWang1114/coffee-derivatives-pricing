# Risk Management Analysis

Scenario 1: Hedging Market Risk

Buy coffee futures to hedge rising purchase costs, assuming the team needs to buy coffee or holds a short position. Match the hedge quantity and maturity to the exposure. Alternatively, buy calls to retain the benefit of lower prices, allowing for the upfront premium.

Use Monte Carlo simulation, VaR, and weather stress tests to compare losses before and after hedging. Monitor prices, volatility, hedge performance, and futures margin requirements, adjusting the hedge when exposure changes.

Scenario 2: Credit Risk with a New Supplier

Assess the supplier's financial statements, payment history, and delivery record. Use credit scoring where suitable data exist, and set an exposure limit reflecting the supplier's finances and regional economic conditions.

Reduce unsecured advance payments through staged payments linked to verified deliveries. Request suitable collateral where feasible and check its value and enforceability. CDS would be relevant only if available protection covers the entity and specified credit events; it does not automatically cover nondelivery.

Diversify suppliers and monitor their finances, delivery performance, and regional conditions. Estimate expected credit loss from default probability, exposure, and loss severity, reducing exposure when risk increases.

Scenario 3: Operational Risk in Trade Execution

Automate routine trade entry and add pre-trade checks for prices, quantities, contract months, duplicate orders, and position limits. Test the system before use and restrict access to authorized staff.

Require independent review of unusual or large trades. Reconcile orders, executed trades, and position records promptly, flagging discrepancies for investigation and correction.

Train staff, document procedures, and maintain audit trails and backup processes. Track error frequency, losses, and resolution times; investigate recurring causes and improve controls.
